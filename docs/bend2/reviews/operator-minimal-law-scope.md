# Operator clarification: minimal inviolables

The operator clarified that laws should enforce a minimal contract of inviolables. Their purpose is to prevent implementation agents from coding banned behaviors into the application while leaving them freedom to design it.

This governs the next review of all existing candidates and Codex's six proposed families. The 138-row extraction provides evidence; its size does not establish the desired law count. The earlier six families need reduction, splitting or rejection under this threshold.

For each candidate, state the exact forbidden behavior, why it must be forbidden in every otherwise valid implementation, and why an existing law does not already cover it. Then challenge scope, exceptions, excluded valid designs and ways the implementation could satisfy the wording while violating the intended prohibition. Only after that consider its Bend encoding and proof.

Architecture, workflows, desirable features and a comprehensive correctness specification require their own justification before any part enters the minimal law set. Keep the smallest independent set that captures the operator's essential boundaries. No laws are approved by this clarification or by the earlier Codex reviews.
