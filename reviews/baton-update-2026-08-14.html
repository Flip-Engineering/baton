The no-clock law, the detached bus, and the uncapped fleet.

Forty-eight hours ago baton could not run two waves at once without wedging; twelve hours ago it could not run a wave past three hours without murdering it; six hours ago the bus answered launches on a 30–60-second delay when it answered at all. All three are gone, and each was removed the same way: measured against live fleet behavior, ruled on by the operator, ripped out at the mechanism, restaged in the suites, and re-proven by a flood of real waves through the changed machinery. This report covers the window: 190 commits, 136 issues filed-to-date (#86–#221), five bootstrap kernel/surface laws landed, one full DSL, and the first 15-pack concurrent flood.

▣The last 6 hours — the operator-ruling era

The law that reframed the era: "timeout/clock based control flows are not appropriate for baton — that is an arbitrary cap/limit, just in general." The trigger was a murder receipt: the honesty-e wave (five members, 4.5h of live work) was stop_completed in sequence at the interpreter's hardCapMs — the wave settled all-failed, harvest all-miss, at the exact second its invisible clock expired. The operator named the class; the mechanism died the same day.

#163 · 8ec52a6c hardCap ripped out everywhere — interpreter loop, wave-driver policy, recipes, production default. A numeric cap now refuses at admission naming the law; the null sentinel is the only accepted form. Waves settle on evidence, never on a clock. The replacement is quiescence-derived completion: the drive exits on terminality, an unrecoverable member, a handled-decision stuck roster, or a roster-wide silence past a window derived from the roster's own observed cadence (max(2×observed gap, 8 polls)) — never elapsed time. Long-running dynamic workflows are now physically possible; before this, every wave carried a hidden self-destruct.
#221 · a3e96e8 The invented seat-ceiling pre-cap ripped out of dispatch. The "1× concurrency" on the two cheapest models was a scaffolding fossil I had been planning around for days. The fleet's real parallelism is the provider's, not ours. The only queue left is provider-true typed backpressure (429/rate_limited, retried, ledgered). Massive cheap-model parallelism (the DSv4Flash swarms) is unthrottled by our own machinery.
#173 · ac0f5bc waves.run detaches at the bus. Launch returns a synchronous acceptance receipt (waveId, manifest digest, roster); the drive runs as a continuation; settlement mints a wave.settled receipt into the store. The bus survives launches. Previously each launch held the serial command loop for the wave's whole life — two live drives wedged every other launch (measured: zero admissions in 43 attempts). Now admission is instant and the mesh moves at launch speed.

The quiescence contract, honestly told. The replacement wasn't improvised: the #163 foundry had already contract→red-team→fold→suite'd the design (14-row red-first suite). Landing it surfaced two contract-level defects the suites' authors couldn't have measured: the phase-stuck terminalization threshold, as written, murdered any member whose in-turn event gap outlived the quiet window (a 400ms in-turn edit died at 120ms — the suite's own cadence rows exposed it), and the survivor-harvest row assumed finalization inside the break poll. Both amendments are documented on the issue and in the suite; the suite is 15/15 green, and the full interpreter/driver battery holds (every remaining failure is a parked red-first row by design). The methodology caught its own contract's bugs before they ate a fleet.

▣What it qualitatively unlocks

∞
wave lifetime

No clock kills work. A wave lives until its members settle, stall handled, or the orchestrator stops it by hand. Long-horizon campaigns are now a first-class shape.

N
fleet width

No invented concurrency ceilings. The fleet is as wide as the providers allow, with typed backpressure as the only queue.

1 doc
per workflow

A dynamic multi-agent workflow is a DSL document with named members, routes, scopes, briefs, and harvest — not a bespoke driver. The orchestrator authors, the interpreter drives.

0 waits
on the bus

Detached launches + the eventsView read path + the single-pass index: the command loop admits, answers, and settles without queueing behind a wave's life.

The composite shift: baton moved from "waves are expensive, fragile, and clock-bombed" to "waves are cheap, concurrent, and die only by evidence." The remaining frontier is no longer whether a fleet can run — it is how honestly the surface reports it, how well members speak, and how the knowledge/plan planes become readable citizens.
