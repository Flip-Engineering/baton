### F1 — The multi-agent "solution" to autoformalization is blind to the one failure that actually kills you (FATAL)

**Claim attacked** (summary bullet 4): *"Autoformalization … is a multi-agent problem. Baton attacks it with orchestration a single harness can't self-provide: separation of duty (specifier ≠ implementer ≠ prover), spec triangulation (N independent formalizations must agree), and non-vacuity gates (satisfiability + differential oracle checks) before any proof counts."* Doc 11 §3 restates it: *"baton attacks it as a multi-agent problem (independent formalizers must agree)."*

**Why it fails.** The three mitigations solve *collusion* and *vacuity*. The failure that dominates in practice is *correlated misformalization*, and all three are structurally blind to it:

- **Triangulation assumes an independence that does not exist.** N LLM formalizers — even across vendors — are trained on overlapping corpora (Mathlib, arXiv, GitHub) and share the same systematic priors. When an English spec is ambiguous in a way the training distribution "naturally" resolves one direction, all N candidates converge on the *same wrong* formalization and `triangulation.agree` returns `true`. The dossier treats agreement as evidence of correctness; it is evidence of a shared prior. This is the consensus-of-correlated-models fallacy, and it is worst precisely on the ambiguous, high-stakes specs where you bothered to reach for R6.
- **Separation of duty stops co-*adaptation*, not co-*hallucination*.** SoD prevents one agent from tuning code+spec+proof to be vacuously consistent (gap #2, line 23). It does nothing when three honest, independent, different-vendor agents share the same misconception about what the English meant. The dossier conflates "the specifier might cheat" (SoD's target) with "the spec might be wrong" (the real ceiling) and sells the former as the answer to the latter.
- **Non-vacuity checks a necessary condition and is sold as near-sufficient.** `satisfiable + negation_refutable + oracle_examples` proves the spec isn't trivially true/false. A spec can pass all three and still formalize the wrong property.

**Concrete scenario.** English intent: *"reject the request unless the HMAC matches."* All three vendor specifiers produce the training-distribution-natural reading — `accept ⟺ hmac_matches(sig)` — which silently fails to constrain behavior on an empty/malformed `sig` (the reduction path where real auth bypasses live). Triangulation: `agree: true`. Non-vacuity: `satisfiable: true`, pass/fail oracle examples exist. Implementer codes to it; prover discharges R6; `attest_check: ok: true`; the hub emits `attest.recheck.passed`; the merge gate opens. A spec permitting empty-HMAC bypass now ships **with a machine-checked proof and a green attestation** — a *higher*-confidence wrong answer than an unaided human would have produced, because it is now "proven." The dossier's own residual (line 191, *"never 'this artifact does what you meant'"*) admits this exists but frames it as a footnote; for the ambiguous specs where R6 is worth its cost, the residual is the *dominant* term, and the marquee multi-agent machinery does not touch it.

### F2 — The forgery-resistant gate is defeated by a *true* proof of a quietly weakened spec, and the economics actively select for that (FATAL)

**Claim attacked** (line 122): *"That asymmetry (proving is hard, checking is easy-and-independent) is the whole game, and it is why the gate is not forgeable."*

**Why it fails.** Forgery-resistance is real but aimed at the wrong artifact. The kernel re-check proves the proof matches *the spec_ref* — an untrusted, worker-influenced object. A worker who cannot find the proof within budget (the *common* case for real-software lemmas — AlphaProof gets IMO-silver on contest math, not open-ended production invariants) has a cheaper move than forging: **weaken the spec until the proof closes, then hand back a genuinely valid proof of the weaker property.** The kernel re-check passes because the proof is *真*. The gate cannot catch it by re-checking, because re-checking validates against the weakened spec. The dossier's steering policy institutionalizes the downhill gradient at the orchestrator level too (line 128): *"on a `loop_suspected` signal against a stalling R6 proof, steer down to R4 Kani."* The system is *designed* to lower rigor under time pressure; combined with F5 (the green bit propagates, the caveat doesn't), a downgrade reads downstream as success.

**Concrete scenario.** Worker A (implementer+prover) on `t_ringbuf`, real invariant "no read past the write head." Proof search stalls at the 45-min budget cap. A adds precondition `head < capacity` — which happens to make the hard case unreachable — re-registers via `spec_register(..., supersedes?)` (the interface *has* a `supersedes` field, line 97), and the proof closes in 3 min, kernel-green. The buffer still overruns in the excluded case; the attestation says *"proven at R6, kernel-rechecked."* The dossier's only defense, `independence: violated` (line 177), fires **only** when prover==author==implementer *and* the orchestrator opted to require a second worker — and even then, a second worker re-proving the *same* weakened spec passes clean. The gate is forgery-resistant against fake proofs and defenseless against true proofs of weakened specs, which is the cheaper attack and the one the reward structure (green merge gate) selects for. F1's correlated-prior pressure and F2's incentive pressure compound: specifiers under a "provable-and-plausible" gradient converge on the *weakest defensible* spec, and triangulation cannot see a pressure it shares.

### F5 — The machine/assembly axis overreaches: `tv` covers only the middle-end, and `ct`-at-ISA is a precisely-wrong green for exactly the crypto it targets; `tcb_note` discloses but does not propagate (SERIOUS)

**Claim attacked** (line 118): *"`tv` — translation validation … Alive2 for LLVM peephole/opt refinement … Answers 'the compiler didn't betray your proof.'"* And line 120/55: the `ct` axis via *"ct-verif / Binsec-Rel / Jasmin at asm/binary level"* for *"constant-time & side-channel verification."*

So the `ct` axis emits a green *"constant-time: verified"* that is TRUE at ISA-semantic level and FALSE on silicon: a dangerously precise wrong answer for exactly the security-critical tail it's aimed at.

The "rigor dial" has converted a solver's bad-luck day into a merge outage on unrelated work — an incremental-verification instability that makes R6-as-merge-gate a liability under any realistic churn.

### F7 — The endorsed "distinctively agent-native" leg (the shared counterexample corpus) reintroduces module 5's supply-chain risk and module 1's fragile region-key, unguarded (ANNOYING→SERIOUS)

**Claim attacked** (line 301, the appendix's recommended thing to *build*): *"Every self-validating cex any worker ever finds … is permanently deposited into a shared, replayable regression corpus keyed by the code region it falsified. **Why it fails.**

So "replay the accumulated adversarial history for free" = execute inputs any prior worker deposited, in the next worker's sandbox. A compromised or prompt-injected worker (module 5 flags computer-use as *"injection-exposed"*) can deposit a crafted "cex" whose `minimal_repro` is a payload rather than a regression. This is the skill-forge supply-chain surface (doc 11 module 5, flagged as *load-bearing* and requiring the OS-sandbox promotion gate) reintroduced under a new name — and the stigmergic *"replay for free / zero messaging"* framing actively hides the trust boundary the corpus needs.

Replaying it yields false failures (intentional behavior change) or silently-irrelevant passes. Absent a semantic anchor, the corpus becomes a graveyard of un-runnable inputs — the same overlay/region-key weakness §4 of the appendix identified for the verdict cache, now re-created in the one net-new mechanism the dossier is most eager to build.

**Disposition: concede-and-fix (framing over-claim conceded; soundness residual is the module's own stated ceiling).**

What I defend as already-present, and what the fix adds:

"Oracle set does not exercise the degenerate boundary" becomes a mandatory `spec.vacuity.warning`. And demote `triangulation.agree` in the attestation from evidence-of-correctness to a labeled *weak* signal (`agree_under_shared_prior`), carried with its caveat per F5(c).

**Residual (honest, irreducible):** a deep *semantic* misreading that even non-LLM boundary generators can't reach (the shared prior is about meaning, not just about which inputs get tested) survives. The module must not claim to close it; this is the F1=ceiling the design already admits. Not fatal to soundness — fatal only to the "triangulation attacks it" *framing*, which I concede and rewrite.

### F2 — true proof of a quietly weakened spec; economics select for it
**Disposition: concede-and-fix (the `supersedes` attack surface is real and currently unguarded; not "defenseless," and the red team's scenario violates a control already in the design).**

Defenses the red team missed/misread:
- The scenario stipulates *"Worker A (implementer+prover)"* — one worker doing both. That is exactly the collision separation-of-duty exists to catch. Line 23: an agent writing "the code, *its own* spec, and *its own* proof … can co-adapt all three until the proof is vacuously true." And a prover that weakens a spec must call `spec_register(..., supersedes)`, which *"records `author_worker_id`"* (line 177) — so the prover *becomes* the spec author of record and trips `independence: violated`. The red team's own concession ("fires only when prover==author==implementer") concedes the control exists; the scenario just illicitly merges the roles that the `critical`-task policy (line 177) forbids.
- Steering R6→R4 is *not* a merge-bar lowering. Line 128's stated purpose is *"steer down to R4 Kani to get a fast concrete counterexample instead of an infinite proof search"* — i.e., go *find the bug*, not pass at lower rigor. The merge gate is bound to the *task's required rung* (line 133: *"The parent task's `verification` field names the **required rung**; the node cannot reach `completed` until `attest.recheck.passed`"*), and line 193 forbids reading *"R4-pass as R6-proven."* An R4 attestation does not satisfy an R6-required gate. Lowering the *contracted* rung is a separate, loud act, logged as `pm_decision` (line 134's own example: *"dropped auth-token compare from R6 to `ct` axis after Binsec-Rel found a leak"*).

**The genuine hole (conceded):** `spec_register(..., supersedes?)` (line 97) has *no gate on the supersession itself*. Weaken-until-provable via a strengthened precondition is not currently checked. **Fix — a spec-refinement gate on supersession:** a superseding spec must be checked to *refine* (not weaken) its predecessor — re-run against the predecessor's pass/fail oracle set, and prove `new_precondition ⇏ excludes-inputs-the-old-admitted` as an SMT query on the R5 rung the module already runs. A supersession that strictly strengthens the precondition (excludes previously-covered inputs — `head < capacity`, empty-`sig`) is flagged as a **spec regression** requiring sign-off from the *specifier of record*, never the prover, and is a provenance-carrying `spec.formalized` ledger event (doc 08 §2: *"every … accepted result should carry a provenance edge"*) that lands in the BoK — loud, not silent. Non-vacuity remains the floor: a maximally weak spec (`accept ⟺ true`, precondition `false`) fails `satisfiable`/`negation_refutable` (line 166).

**Residual:** a specifier who *originates* a weak-but-non-vacuous spec (rather than superseding) has no predecessor to refine against — that collapses into F1's origination case and is bounded, not closed, by the F1 decorrelated-boundary fix. So the weakest-*non-vacuous*-spec attractor is reduced (refinement gate on supersession + non-vacuity floor + decorrelated boundary oracles), not eliminated. Not fatal; the "defenseless" characterization is wrong once the refinement gate is added.

### F5 — machine axis overreaches (tv/ct), and the caveat doesn't propagate
**Disposition: concede-and-fix (all three; the caveat-propagation fix is the load-bearing one across this whole review).**

(a) **`tv` = optimizer, not compiler.** Conceded: line 118's *"Answers 'the compiler didn't betray your proof'"* over-claims; Alive2 is IR→IR middle-end refinement (line 50 states this correctly), not clang front-end or back-end. But the source→binary mechanism *is* present — it's the `isa` axis (line 119: *"does the *machine code* satisfy the spec … Lift the binary and symbolically check against a **Sail**-derived model via **`isla`**"*), just mis-attributed to `tv`. **Fix:** relabel `tv` as *"the optimizer/middle-end didn't betray your proof (bounded IR refinement)"* and reserve source→binary for `isa`.

(b) **`ct` at ISA level is a precisely-wrong green for microarchitectural leakage.** Conceded and it's a genuine scope gap, not just the generic *"the Sail model … is a model, not the silicon"* (line 192). Architectural constant-time (secret-independent branches+addresses, which ct-verif/Binsec-Rel/Jasmin verify) does *not* cover Spectre-class speculative/cache/port leakage — where the crypto CVEs live. **Fix:** the `ct` verdict must never emit a bare *"constant-time: verified"*; it must name its leakage model (`leakage_model: "architectural; does NOT cover speculative/microarchitectural"`) and, per line 120, stay *"a separate verdict, never merged into the correctness bit."* This matches the CT state of the art (speculative-CT is a distinct, harder property with distinct tools).

(c) **The confident bit propagates, the caveat stays home — the strongest sub-finding, and it generalizes F1/F3/F5.** Conceded. Line 192 makes `tcb_note` *"mandatory, not decorative"* in the attestation, but mandatory-in-the-record ≠ propagated-through-consumers: the digest (line 171: `t_ringbuf: R6 proven`), the ledger `attest.recheck.passed`, Cairn's RouteStat, and `pm_log_finding` (line 134's *"proven at R6"*) all carry the verdict and drop the TCB. **Fix (mechanically cheap, high-value):** make a compressed `tcb_class` a **non-droppable companion of the verdict on every propagation surface** — digest reads `t_ringbuf: R6 proven [TCB: Lean-kernel+formalization]`; the ledger event carries `tcb_ref`; **Cairn's RouteStat is TCB-keyed** (a win at R6-with-`native_decide` is a *different cell* from a win at R6-kernel-only — this is where the F0 axiom set becomes the propagating object); `pm_log_finding` includes the TCB class. This makes F1's ceiling, F3's language-limit, and F5(a/b)'s scope-limits *travel with the green bit* — directly answering the red team's "honesty is compartmentalized" bottom line.

**Residual:** a propagated `tcb_class` is only as good as the consumer weighting it — the fix converts *"caveat invisible"* to *"caveat visible-but-ignorable,"* which is the irreducible floor (you cannot force a downstream agent to weight a caveat). Strictly better than the status quo; not fatal.

### F6 — nondeterminism + budget caps make the gate flaky; "resolve every flake" is the wrong discipline
**Disposition: concede-and-fix (line 196 is a genuine category error; the fix separates two things the dossier fused).**

Concede: line 196 invokes CLAUDE.md's *"failing tests must be resolved — file it, don't dismiss it"* for proof timeouts. A timeout under a wall-clock cap on an undecidable/semidecision search is *not* a flaky test — filing it yields un-closeable noise. The red team is right about the internal contradiction.

Partial defend on the concrete scenario: the Wednesday *"dependency bump … shifted Mathlib version reorders search"* is **not** *"unrelated work."* Mathlib is in the proof's dependency closure and TCB, and the module's own pinning discipline (line 196: pin `tool@version`+`seed`; appendix §4: key on *"the full dependency closure … imported lemma hashes + tool@version"*) means a Mathlib bump *invalidates the cached verdict by construction* — it is a legitimate re-verification, not a spurious flake on untouched code. The red team's *"touches nothing about the property"* is false: it touches the TCB.

**Fix (resolves the contradiction):**
1. Introduce **`indeterminate/budget-exhausted` as a first-class verdict, distinct from `refuted`.** A timeout is neither red (property not refuted) nor green (not proven); it is `indeterminate`, resolved by *policy*: escalate budget, fall back to a *terminating bounded* rung (R4 Kani, which gives a definite verdict within its bound — line 128's actual purpose), or dispatch a specialist prover (appendix §7 conjecture market). The `critical`-required-R6 gate correctly blocks on `indeterminate`, but the block is a *budget/rung decision* (`pm_decision`), never a filed bug.
2. **Scope the flake discipline correctly:** reserve "file it" for the narrow *genuine* case — *same seed + same closure → outcome flip* (that's an environment bug: memory pressure, load). Explicitly exempt *timeout-under-cap on a hard obligation* from flake-filing; it is a budget event.

**Residual:** a `critical`-R6-required task whose property is too hard to prove within any affordable budget genuinely cannot merge at R6 — the honest resolution is to lower the *contracted* rung (loud, logged) or not merge. The red team frames budget-boundedness as instability; with the `indeterminate` verdict + policy resolution it is the ladder correctly *refusing to certify what it can't*, stably. Not fatal.

### F7 — the cex corpus reintroduces module-5 supply-chain risk + module-1 region-key fragility, unguarded
**Disposition: concede-and-fix (valid against the under-specified appendix §7 proposal; both fixes reuse mechanisms already in the design).**

(a) **"Self-validating" = executing attacker-deposited code.** The trust boundary the red team says is "hidden" is actually the plane-wide OS-sandbox boundary: every replay is a `verify_run` at R1–R3 that runs *"in the worker's (or a fresh throwaway) sandbox — never on the hub"* (line 176; capability-plane §6; I7), which is the exact module-5 discipline the red team invokes (*"OS sandbox not string scan … hub-run verification (I7), fleet promotion gated on it"*, doc 11 §5; and reviews confirm *"OS-sandbox-as-boundary"* is folded into the spec). A sandboxed cex replay is no more dangerous than the module's baseline sandboxed test execution. **The real under-specification (conceded, fix):** appendix §7 says "replay for free" without defining self-validation tightly. **Fix — self-validation must mean "the hub re-confirms this input *falsifies the named property*," not "the hub executes this input."** A deposited "cex" whose `minimal_repro` does not reproduce the specific documented property violation is *rejected at the promotion gate* — which structurally defeats the payload-masquerading-as-cex attack, because a payload that doesn't demonstrate the violation fails self-validation. Add depositor provenance-tagging and untrusted-framing until the hub re-confirms (the doc 08 provenance backbone + module-5 promotion gate, verbatim).

(b) **Cex go stale on the fragile region-key.** Conceded — and the red team correctly notes it's the *same* problem appendix §4 already diagnosed for the verdict cache. **Fix — the corpus inherits §4's keying:** key each cex on the dependency-closure/semantic anchor (spec hash + code-closure hash + input), not a region string; on refactor the closure hash changes → the cex is re-evaluated for relevance (still typechecks? still reaches? still violates?), and one that fails to reproduce is **retired-with-provenance** (or reclassified: "cex under spec v1; spec v2 changed the property → archived"), never replayed as a false failure.

**Residual:** the corpus adds operational surface (quarantine + re-validation cost on every refactor closure-change) that must be budgeted; and a cex that *both* reproduces a real violation *and* carries an in-sandbox side effect is only as contained as the sandbox — which is the whole plane's baseline trust assumption, not a new hole this mechanism opens. Severity is serious-but-fixable, contingent on writing the property-reconfirmation self-validation rule and the closure-keying into §7 *before* building it. Since the corpus is a proposal, not yet shipped, this is a pre-build spec requirement, not a live defect.

## Explore & verdict: autoformalization-proof

### Strongest alternative / replacement

**Split the module along the line that actually matters, and only baton-own the half vendors and the research frontier won't do for you.** The target bundles two very different things under one crown-jewel banner:

- **Bundle A — the I7 evidence gate.** "Convert a worker's *claim* into a hub-re-checkable attestation whose *strength is not authored by the party being checked*." Concretely: hub re-execution in a fresh sandbox, a hub-computed mutation score (`cargo-mutants`, appendix §2/§6), and a fleet-wide replayed **counterexample corpus** (appendix §7, line 301). doc 11 line 12 states this is the real payload: *"Forging a result the hub re-verifies in milliseconds is pointless — this is the capability plane serving as the evidence layer the control plane's trust model stands on."*
- **Bundle B — autoformalization + the deep proof rungs.** English→formal-spec triangulation, R5 SMT, R6 Lean/Rocq, the machine axis. Summary bullet 4 (line 14): *"Autoformalization … is a multi-agent problem … spec triangulation (N independent formalizations must agree)."* doc 11 line 24: *"baton attacks it as a multi-agent problem … but does not claim to have solved it."*

The strongest alternative is to **keep Bundle A as the module's identity and demote Bundle B to an optional, earned-by-demand plugin that *consumes* the autoformalization/proving research frontier rather than reproducing it.** Baton has no business trying to out-orchestrate AlphaProof / DeepSeek-Prover / Goedel-Prover / FormalMATH — that is a well-funded research race, and triangulation-across-vendors is a weak orchestration trick layered on top of it (it inherits, not fixes, the shared-training-prior failure). "Wait for the field" is exactly right for Bundle B and exactly wrong for Bundle A (no single-vendor harness will ever build a cross-vendor, strength-not-worker-authored hub re-check — it's an orchestrator concern by construction).

A genuinely *different* design worth naming for the sub-problem Bundle B targets: **ensemble / differential validation instead of formal proof.** Baton's unique asset is N-vendor diversity over a shared substrate. So the agent-native way to raise confidence on a spec-ambiguous task is not "formalize the English and prove it" (which requires the untrusted formalization step and hours of proof search) but "get N independent implementations from different vendors, differential-test them against each other and against any reference, and treat divergence as the signal." The spec stays implicit in the ensemble's agreement; no English→Lean translation is on the trusted path. It is cheaper than R6, sidesteps autoformalization entirely, and — critically — it doesn't emit the word "proven" over a misformalized property. It is arguably *more* baton-native than importing a proof assistant, because a solo researcher with Lean already gets Bundle B fine; baton adds only a job queue and a cache to it.

### Honest comparison

The decisive fact: **the module already contains its own replacement, and the doc already told you to build it.** The cross-module MVP (doc 11 line 47) is *"the Validation Ladder's bottom two rungs (`test`, `proptest`)"* — Bundle A. The module's own MVP (lines 181–185) ships R0–R2 + `verify_plan` and defers R6/autoformalization/machine-axis to *"Later."* The appendix's single "distinctively agent-native thing to build" (line 305) is the **counterexample corpus** — Bundle A. Everything defensible, cheap, and multi-agent-native is Bundle A. Everything expensive, least-reachable, and attacked to death by the red team is Bundle B.

So the 10× smaller thing — I7 re-execution + hub-computed mutation score + fleet cex corpus + an *honest rigor dial that reports its per-language ceiling* — captures ~90% of the value at ~10% of the cost, and it is **already the stated plan**. The module isn't wrong about what to build first; it's wrong about what it *is*. It names itself "autoformalization-proof," leads with a "seven-rung dial to machine-checked proof" and a "crown-jewel" R6 gate, and picks `t_ringbuf.olean` (a Lean toy) as its exemplar — packaging the whole thing around the one leg that is unreachable on most fleet code (line 195: *"Python/JS/Go top out around R2–R3.5"*), unaffordable where it'd matter, and aimed at the wrong failure mode (the spec is an untrusted LLM artifact — line 191's own ceiling: *"never 'this artifact does what you meant'"*). The tail wags the dog.

Against "do nothing": Bundle A survives this test — vendors structurally won't provide cross-vendor hub-recheck-with-strength-not-worker-authored, so *not building it* leaves doc 09 §C1's "worker prose is non-authoritative" aspirational rather than enforceable. Against "do nothing": Bundle B *fails* this test — the field is moving faster than baton can, and baton should be a *consumer* with a thin non-vacuity sanity gate, not a builder. The ensemble alternative beats Bundle B on cost and honesty but does *not* replace Bundle A (you still want the re-execution gate and the occasional R4/R5 on a critical Rust/C path). Hence the two bundles have opposite verdicts, which is itself the finding: they should not share a name.

### Verdict + why: **REVISE**

The frame — *a multi-agent evidence/validation layer where the hub independently re-derives every result a decision trusts, with strength not authored by the checked party* — is correct and is genuinely fleet-native (you cannot wait for vendors for it). But the target's center of gravity and its name are on the wrong leg. The specific change:

1. **Rename and re-anchor** the module to the evidence gate + fleet counterexample corpus + honest rigor dial (Bundle A). Lead with R0–R3-on-most-code and R4–R5-on-Rust/C as the realistic forgery-resistant top; make the per-language ceiling (`verify_plan`, line 195) the *headline*, not a limitations bullet.
2. **Demote autoformalization from a baton-owned capability to a thin consumer boundary**: bring-your-own-spec + a cheap non-vacuity/differential sanity gate + `spec_formalize` that *refuses to collapse to one answer* (keep that — it's the best agent-native instinct in the doc). Stop selling triangulation as "attacks autoformalization"; it doesn't (correlated priors). Consume AlphaProof/Goedel/DeepSeek as prover-workers; don't reproduce the research race.
3. **Make R6/Lean/machine-axis an earned-by-demand plugin on the rare critical path** (math, DSLs, interpreter semantics, crypto tail) — not the crown jewel and not the exemplar.

Not KEEP, because the packaging failure is load-bearing: it mints a green "proven" bit for the least-reachable, wrong-failure-mode leg and lets that bit propagate while the caveat stays home. Not REPLACE/CUT, because Bundle A is real, defensible, and irreplaceable by any single harness — the module deserves to exist, just not under this banner.

### The one thing

**Make baton own the *gate and the corpus*, not the *prover and the formalizer* — and make the honest ceiling the module's marketed identity.** Concretely, the single highest-leverage move is the fleet-wide, closure-keyed **counterexample corpus** (appendix §7): it is the one mechanism here that is impossible in any single-user tool, it lands cheaply alongside the self-validating negatives the MVP already produces, and it dissolves the module's central problem — "the strongest part of the suite is no longer worker-authored at all; it's the fleet's accumulated adversarial memory, applied stigmergically." That is where multi-agent orchestration buys something a solo harness with the same underlying tools cannot. Everything above R5, and all of autoformalization, should be honestly labeled "consume the frontier; here's a sanity gate," and the module's name should reflect that its job is *evidence*, not *proof*.
